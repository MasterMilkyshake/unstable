# Unstable — The Shattered Coast

> A world can survive a catastrophe and still be destroyed by the way people explain it.

**Setting:** The Shattered Coast. **Present:** Year 113 After the Fracture (AF). This is the author-facing setting bible; it contains discoveries deliberately hidden from a new character. The optional journal lives in `src/data/chronicle.js`, its physical sources in `src/data/lore.js`, and its responsive testimony in `src/data/testimonies.js`.

## 1. The promise of the setting

You wake with salt in your lungs. Someone gives you a blanket before asking your name. Beyond the fire are ferries, hungry animals, people who will help you, and people who will kill you for a meal. Beneath all of them is an arrangement that once let a coast share the work of surviving. That arrangement broke. Its commands did not stop.

Unstable is about **the difference between enduring and being cared for**. A machine may endure for a century. A knight may obey beyond death. A city may preserve a law while amending away its meaning. The player becomes powerful enough to interrupt these arrangements, then learns that interruption and repair are different accomplishments.

Combat should be demanding, journeys consequential, and knowledge useful. Take inspiration from the tension of exploring a hostile action RPG; build an original identity around public works, memory, debt, and the people left carrying burdens after those who assigned them are gone.

### Narrative commitments

- A person is never reducible to a race, a faction, or a quest marker.
- Useful institutions can commit real harm. Their failures do not make their work worthless.
- A source has an author, an audience, and something it cannot know.
- A surprising revelation should explain two earlier details, not erase them.
- Competence is attractive. A cook, mechanic, hunter, or lamplighter can be as consequential as a warrior.
- Death costs something. Losing the last life permanently ends the character and their discoveries.
- Leave some uncertainty honest. The Hollow Man's name is not revealed simply because the player wants closure.
- Put the ordinary before the cosmic: a missing tool, an empty chair, an unpaid crew, then the mechanism connecting them.

## 2. What the world is

The **Anchorage** was a connected coastal shelf with upland settlements. Its people built a distributed lattice of regulators to manage the shelf's movement and the pressure beneath it. The **Anchorage Compact** described how stations shared the load. No single station was meant to possess the others.

Four surviving kinds of works explain the geography:

| Works | Function | Living consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern regulator | Stores strain as arrested motion and cold | Frosthollow freezes beyond its intended boundary |
| Southern furnace | Releases stored strain as heat | Ashen Spire pulses, its overflow galleries increasingly neglected |
| Marsh channels | Distribute pressure through living water | Shimmerfen needs continuous, poorly understood maintenance |
| Rootworks | Absorb slow strain through broad living networks | Verdance's groves are both homes and infrastructure |

A **command chain** linked their signals to the keep below the shelf. The chain was a protocol and a physical structure, not a divine object holding islands from a literal ceiling. Ancient artists pictured a crowned figure bearing a chain; later rulers encouraged the picture because a shared system was easier to own if people believed one king held it up.

The **Rift** is the exposed break where the physical lattice intersects the passage of the dead. Its geometry is navigable locally but unreliable at larger scales. At its north-east lip the passage's last carrier, the Lanternless, still keeps a landing that his crossings never reached; past it the horizon is suggestive rather than measurable.

The setting does not establish a creator god, a true universal religion, or a definitive reason the passage and the lattice intersect. Characters offer theories. Do not promote one to fact casually.

### The shape of the Anchorage

Every island is the remains of something people built, which is why the map can be read as history. **Compass convention:** +z is north and +x is **west** in world coordinates (the HUD compass and the map agree).

| Place | Direction from Saltreach | What it was | What it is now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saltreach | centre | The old high country, first dry ground after the break | The Lantern City: walled harbor, Keepers' Terrace, Sage's Rise, Millbrook farms, Kestrel Heights, Greywood |
| Shimmerfen | east (same landmass) | Marsh outlets of the lattice | Reed villages and the Covenant's channels, reached on foot by the Reed Road (no port of its own); the Hundred Arches stride out of the Fenwall's western spur over the Arch Mere, in sight of the Wash, to Hightoll on its rock at their north end |
| Frosthollow | north-east | Northern regulator | Permanent winter around the Warden |
| Ashen Spire | south, past the Wash (the end of Thornwood and Meridian's continent) | Southern furnace | The Colossus's caldera and its forgotten galleries above the Emberwood, which runs on into Meridian's lowland |
| The Verdance | north | Rootworks forest | Mossbound groves, Kingsfall |
| Thornwood & Meridian | west (one continent, with the Ashen Spire at its southern end) | Upland road; the Compact's customs bastion on the western sea gate | Redwake forest; Fort Meridian, rebuilt by the returned crown fleet |
| The Hush | north-east, on Frosthollow's southern shore | Vahl listening station that measured the shelf | The Ministry of Soundings and its Quiet Archive, under Frosthollow's spreading winter |

Rules for adding to the map: a place exists because someone built, maintained, fled or fought over it; roads connect landings to the places people actually walk to; guards stand where an institution has something to protect; written records sit on objects that would carry them (books on lecterns, notices on posts, carvings on stone), never on free-standing glowing tablets.

## 3. The Fracture: authorial truth

During a shelf failure, the keep requested an emergency release. The release would flood the royal dry docks and permit a slower redistribution of the coast. The last ruler to hold the title **First Anchor** withheld the signal until the crown vessels could escape.

The stations took a load they could not carry. The command chain broke. Coastland sank. The seams of the exposed system lit the sky, creating the visible crack. The death passage opened into the damaged world, and the sea began returning people without a complete memory of themselves.

The last Anchor persisted in the Rift as the **Hollow Duke**. His command no longer controlled the system, but its residual claim continued drawing pressure to his throne. The Duke fed on that pressure to remain coherent. A former public obligation became a private survival mechanism.

Maren's grandmother copied the refusal as the signal room flooded. Maren inherited both the copy and a civic tradition of hiding it. She did not cause the original disaster. She is responsible for continuing the concealment and for sending others into danger under an incomplete explanation.

Defeating the Duke interrupts his claim. It quiets the tides. It does not restore the old coast, replace dead workers, or automatically absolve Saltreach's leaders.

## 5. The laws of power

**Mana** is pressure in passage through the broken lattice. **Ether** is a living being's capacity to shape that passage. Ilvar's training teaches a person to hold mana deliberately. A mantra is a repeatable combination of breath, motion, attention, and learned structure. Its syllables help teaching; no entire people's language is secretly the one true spell language.

| Attunement | Principle | Cultural disagreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emberweave | Release as heat | Warmth shared with a household versus fire treated as proof of authority |
| Rimebind | Arrest motion | Preservation as care versus preservation without consent |
| Stormcall | Conduct difference between points | Connection versus command at a distance |
| Galesong | Redirect motion | A route open to others versus escape purchased alone |
| Umbracast | Work along absence left by the break | Making room for the lost versus turning absence into a weapon |

These disagreements are thematic interpretations, not hidden elemental alignment rules. Every attunement is available across kindreds when mechanical requirements are met.

**Talents** are practiced habits made reliable under danger. **Power** measures developed capacity, not social rank or moral worth. **Mantra Sparks** stabilize a learned form so it can be strengthened. The **Shrine of Order** redistributes trained investment; it does not rewrite a person's life.

### The nine Vows

The Vowkeeper preserves forms of deliberate self-binding that enough people survived to pass on. A Vow is permanent in current play. Its explicit gifts and restrictions must always be visible before commitment.

| Vow | Tradition | Practical identity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hollow | Refuse an outside claimant to the soul | Flexible offense and ether; refusal is still a binding shape |
| Galeblade | Never make captivity a home | Air movement and aerial attacks |
| Silence | Let the trained body speak for itself | Physical force; attunement mantras closed |
| Seer | Attend before judging | Perception, parries, healing |
| Arc | Receive force without passing it to the helpless | Posture, wards, projectile reflection; adherents are Arcwarders |
| Dawn | Bear witness to the returning light | Daylight and lasting flame |
| Abyss | Look into the break without abandoning attention | Rift endurance and power at the edge of sanity |
| Contract | Make the price of violence explicit | Bounties, silver, marked targets; deeply contested ethics |
| Blades | Bind improvement to edge and arm | Weapon criticals and posture pressure |

Ideals can be interpreted or betrayed in fiction. Current code does not contain a secret oath-breaking morality system. Do not imply that poetic obligations are additional undocumented mechanics.

## 6. Death and return

A **tide-born** is a returned person, not a thirteenth kindred. The sea can return any kindred. A chosen name has standing in Saltreach even when an old one is lost. Wren's refusal to attach a body description to a chosen name is intentional: the archive must not become an authority that tells survivors who they are permitted to be.

A combat defeat first leaves the player knocked. An enemy may execute them. Surface death leads to the Rift. The Ascent offers return: through the keep's gate hall and out its north door, one stone lift still runs from the courtyard to the summit, where the Wound lets the living out; the pale gates have closed. Death in the Rift cuts every name-knot at once: there is no return from the place the dead pass through. Fleeing in danger frays one knot. At zero knots the character is gone.

Keepers call lives **name-knots**, remaining ties between a person and a world that remembers them. A tide-born begins with three. An Ember of Life repairs one lost tie. This is a cultural explanation of the mechanic, not a proven physics equation.

Journal sources, investigations and field notes belong to the active character and are lost when their final life is spent. Starting a new character explicitly replaces that saved journey.

**The register of the lost.** When a tide-born dies in the Rift, or its last name-knot frays, Wren strikes the name in her blanket register; she does not erase it. The coast keeps a little of what that person did: **Remembrance**, which the next soul to wash ashore can draw on (a different shore to wake on, a bundle from Wren's stores, an ember kept back). A lost character may leave one thing in the **keepsake chest** by Wren's fire, tied with a knot of pale cord, the register's mark for a struck name; the next newcomer may take it once. This is the coast's custom of passing on what the dead carried, not a promise that anyone returns. (Mechanically: the Register of the Lost on the main menu, shared by every save slot; core/legacy.js.)

**What the dead carry.** Whoever falls drops part of what they carried where they fell, and the coast's custom is that a pouch on the ground belongs to whoever opens it. The Keepers have always run a strongbox at every inn they license, one ledger for the whole coast: what a lodger leaves in their care is outside the sea's reach. This is the same administration that keeps shelter registers and ferry stores (section 8), and the strongbox is the part of it nobody argues with.

The Hollow Man is a stranded creature with damaged temporal testimony. His scratched tally is not a reliable chronology of the Rift. Undead knights and regulators also do not obey all rules of living tide-born bodies.

Sanity measures the ability to maintain a coherent self amid the Rift's competing signals. Low sanity creates perceptual danger, not a moral defect. Ordinary fear and grief are not signs of corruption.

## 7. Peoples and households

The kindreds predate the Fracture. Their physical gifts are real; their cultures are varied and changeable. A family tradition is an invitation to characterization, never an instruction that every member behave alike.

| Kindred | A living practice | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ashkin | Carry a hearth coal between households | Making warmth imply universal kindness |
| Hollowfang | Preserve routes in responsive songs | Treating clans as instinct without reason |
| Vahl | Teach listening with cliff stones | Making mysticism exclusive to their blood |
| Morrow | Mark witnessed memory separately from inherited recitation | Infallible racial memory |
| Glimmerkin | Hear regulator faults through resonant crystal veins | Treating people as components |
| Velani | Maintain seasonal shelter traditions from before permanent winter | One frozen, unchanging culture |
| Kithari | Carry news along rope roads and informal routes | Reducing them to thieves or animal jokes |
| Stonebrood | Sign foundations hidden from patrons | Assuming strength means obedience or lack of imagination |
| Saberan | Record obligations survived in scars | Counting worth only in kills |
| Lumenite | Keep lamp circles during sanity storms | Equating visible light with moral purity |
| Starborn | Begin oral histories with falling cinders and unfamiliar skies | Definitively explaining their origin without evidence |
| Drakeling | Preserve images of extinct sky-fauna | Promising a living dragon dynasty as established fact |

Ports have mixed households, adopted children, former rivals sharing work, and migrants who keep several traditions. Mossbound is a covenant membership, not a race. Tide-born is a condition, not ancestry.

## 8. The factions as institutions

### Saltreach Keepers

Saltreach calls its king the Regent. He holds the Lantern Palace in the Anchor's name until there is an Anchor worth the name; the Keepers govern for him, and Elder Maren is their public voice.

**Need:** a functioning refuge. **Resources:** wells, shelter registers, ferry stores, administrative legitimacy. **Public claim:** keep the lamp lit and the gates watched. **Failure:** treating truth and shelter as dispensable under shortage. **Internal split:** protect the institution's reputation or distribute the records that would let residents correct it. Maren embodies that conflict, rather than secretly serving a cosmic enemy.

### The Watch

**Need:** safe crossings and paid patrols. **Resources:** drill yards, armor, harbor dues, local trust. **Failure:** enforcing a debt arrangement that helped make its own enemies. Hale is sincere, limited, and capable of admitting the withheld wages. He should not become a caricature the moment the ledger is found.

### Redwake crews

**Need:** boats and wages after dispossession. **Resources:** seamanship, a stockade, stolen supplies, Kaelen's command fragment. **Failure:** making passengers pay for the harbor's wrongdoing. Kaelen's own handwriting records both the injustice against his crews and their uncompensated thefts. Keep both pages.

### Reed Covenant

**Need:** functioning pressure outlets. **Resources:** local ecological knowledge, attunement practice, living reeds. **Failure:** secrecy and violence that destroy trust with the families it also protects. Some wardens leave fish as restitution; others endorse letting outsiders suffer to prove their usefulness. Neither side speaks for all members.

### White Cult

**Need:** certainty against loss. **Resources:** frost magic, access to the slopes, a compelling promise of preservation. **Failure:** removing consent and life in the name of preventing decay. The Warden is their object of devotion, not necessarily a being that requested worship.

### Cinder Witnesses and zealots

**Origin:** furnace attendants who kept the eastern works safe. **Need:** a role that makes suffering meaningful. **Failure:** replacing maintenance knowledge with sacrificial spectacle. Ignis can recover the distinction between working in service of the furnace and feeding people into it.

### Mossbound covenant

**Need:** named dead, living groves, legible boundaries. **Resources:** household obligations, masks, root knowledge, defenders. **Failure:** defending a real archive through reprisals that become incomprehensible to outsiders. Ama's settlers have real needs too; the mask bounty is nevertheless an injury the player can choose to perform. The game must allow evidence to criticize a rewarded action.

### Drowned Order

**Need:** relief from an obligation without an ending. **Resources:** discipline, armor, an oath that survived death. **Failure:** obedience severed from reception of new information. The undelivered relief order establishes that someone tried to save them. It does not make the attempt effective after the fact.

### The Enclave

**Origin:** the crown ships that escaped because the last Anchor withheld the release. **Need:** a coast that answers when called, and a claim that makes their century at sea mean something. **Resources:** warships, the Peacekeepers (soldiers sent wherever the returned wash up), Fort Meridian and Meridian Harbour, the Registry of Returned Persons, the Anchor's seal. **Public claim:** order and protection for the counted. **Failure:** treating people as property of whoever counts them, and repeating the centralisation that drowned the coast. **Ranks:** Grand Warden, Warden, Vice Warden, then commanders, captains and the Peacekeepers. The Warden titles are the regulator Wardens' own, taken to claim their authority over the old works (the Warden in the White Hollow is the original, and no part of the Enclave). **Leadership:** Aurek Vell, the Grand Warden (believes the withheld release was correct); the Warden, who keeps the fleet at sea and is not yet met; Varga Thorne, the Vice Warden, who holds Fort Meridian with the central ranks; Commander Rell of the fort; Captain Thessaly Crane, the envoy. Peacekeepers do not attack registered people unless the Enclave is at war with them.

### The Ministry of Soundings

**Origin:** the Compact office that measured the shelf at the Hush's listening stones. **Need:** to stop the passage that returns people with less of themselves each time. **Resources:** a century of soundings, the Quiet Archive, the Sounding House bell, discreet operatives. **Public claim:** none; the Ministry rarely makes public claims. **Failure:** secrecy, and a recommendation (close the passage) that would end every future return without asking anyone who might return. **Leadership:** Minister Hesper Quill; the Grey Clerk; Brother Wick, a boatman who records dissent.

### Factions in motion

The institutions are not furniture. Enclave columns march the roads between forts and posts and fight whatever they meet; at low standing, a checkpoint stops a traveler for a toll, a Writ, or a fight. Anyone wanted too long is followed by the faction's hunters until the fine is paid or the water lies between them, and the Enclave's cutters chase the wanted at sea. Each officer keeps a board of contracts that turns over daily. Standing buys trust in steps: an officer's own stock, a faction's outfit and token, and at the top something no one else carries. Favour with one can cool another: the Enclave and the Ministry pull against each other, with the Watch between. The story's choices (the Writ answered or refused, the harbor taken or held, the Seat broken, sealed or claimed) change who patrols, who stops you, and who holds the coast.

### The Lanternless

An institution collapsed into a being: carrier, vessel, toll collector and hoard. The toll once identified a traveler for a landing. The route failed while the collection continued. He is not a general death god and does not own every soul in the world.

## 9. Regions as places to live

Each existing region has an unlockable Atlas dossier covering history, daily life, threats and leads. Recommended Power is advice, not a gate.

**The Wash:** salvage economy, repaired cups, drying blankets. Wren's first question should concern immediate survival. A wreck log gives the player reason to suspect the disaster was directed by a broken passage rather than bad seamanship.

**Saltreach:** the setting's emotional home. Lamp oil, soup, laundry, hinges and wage arguments make it worth returning to. The lighthouse is a navigation service before it is a duel arena. The charter and its amendment turn a piece of civic furniture into a political history.

**Thornwood:** a drowned road's uplands. Sailor habits survive inside a brigand camp. Ruins preserve a Compact account without a crown. The player learns that institutions and rebels can both describe themselves truthfully while omitting different harms.

**Shimmerfen:** an inhabited maintenance landscape. Reeds, nets and effigies occupy the same channels. A basket of clean fish mistaken for a curse is a stronger expression of distrust than a paragraph saying two factions dislike one another.

**Movement and height:** the Anchorage was built in tiers: shelves, terraces, sea walls and the stepped faces of the old works. People climb them by their ledges, catching an edge, hanging and leaping to the next, not by crawling up sheer rock; a face with no ledges is a wall. Where no road or ledge climbs, crews strung **carrier lines**: Hightoll's garrison across the Arches' breaks, sailors' rigging from the Wash's tower to the Prospect, haul lines that draw a rider uphill.

**Hightoll:** a post that never stood down. The Compact's waystation where the viaduct came ashore, crowned by the Anchors with a keep and a toll; when the spans fell its Bridge Gate opened on nothing. The garrison strung carrier lines across the breaks to pass reports pier to pier, and the castellan, Odric, still keeps the gate for a relief that was ordered by the road. The crack register on the gate answers the service plate on span eight. His bell rings at evening while the post is kept; defeating him lets it fall still, and relieves no one.

**Frosthollow:** care separated from consent. Hunters mend garments while cultists promise an end to change. Empty tool hooks show how a neglected service problem became a boss encounter.

**Ashen Spire:** endurance mistaken for divine preference. The Colossus's old plaque is moving because nobody intended to relieve it. The service instructions sharpen that account: a crew was supposed to exist. Ignis's measurements make a scientific witness consequential.

**The Verdance:** names maintained through replacement rather than frozen intact. Masks rot; their names move to new wood through collective memory. The Old King is a Primadon integrated into an ecology, not another deposed human monarch.

**The Rift:** commands without context. The keep is both a military site and a communication failure. Pale exit gates and camps should be learned as survival landmarks before players invest in the throne's explanation.

**Meridian:** order offered as protection. Papers open doors; their absence closes them. The Registry ledger, the empty cages on Gallows Rock and the Grand Warden's log let the player judge the Enclave from its own records.

**The Hush:** measurement as grief. Fog, chairs that face the sea, stones that listen, a bell that rings for an answer that never came. The Ministry is courteous and exact, and its recommendation is terrible. It shares a coast with Frosthollow, and the regulator's cold has come down over its moor: rime on the heather, snow on the Ministry's slate, ice skinning the tarns. The Hush is grey-blue where Frosthollow is white, and the fog is still its own.

**The Landing That Never Came:** unfinished transport. Small lights imply particular people. Soundings do not provide reliable distance. The erased manifest entry leaves a factual gap that remains after the boss can be defeated.

## 11. Everyday texture for future writing

These are writing conventions and expansion material, not promises of additional simulated systems.

**Food:** bread and fish carry harbor life; stews stretch supplies; hunters preserve warmth and calories together. A rare feast is measured by how many people can be invited, not by a fantasy ingredient list.

**Money:** silver buys labor and transport. Rift shards are useful below because they retain coherence there. A merchant accepting one does not therefore worship the Duke. Wages, debts and repayment should have concrete objects attached: boat, roof, rope, medicine.

**Signals:** a returning boat, fog, and an answer from below have different bell calls in sailor tradition. The old relief bell turns a familiar safety signal into the central political failure. These signals currently appear as descriptive lore, not an implemented audio code.

**Funerals:** name a person in the register, leave a chair for a defined period, lend a name to a carved mask, or pass a coal to a household that lost its firekeeper. Traditions differ and can overlap. Do not make every funeral a spectacle of magic.

**Literacy:** records include tally cuts, service diagrams, masks, knots and copied text. A comprehensive archive should not suggest only people who write formal prose preserve history.

**Language:** use clear dialogue with differences in rhythm and attention. Avoid invented apostrophes as a substitute for culture. Technical terms originate in jobs and are later appropriated by religion or government.

**Weather:** the sky crack and irregular moon are experienced facts; their complete cosmology is not settled. People still forecast the next trip using wind and water. A frightening sky need not produce a prophecy every night.

**Celebrations:** future stories can use lamp relighting, launching a repaired ferry, or the shared carving of a replacement mask. Build an occasion around work completed together and let disagreement survive the celebration.

## 12. Ecology and encounter meaning

Mudlurkers exploit warm shallows and discharge pools. Scuttlers turn dangerous shorelines into feeding grounds. Gloomowls nest alone in abandoned high structures, each three times a person's height and holding its ground by itself; thunderwings are storm-touched relatives. Sharkos walked up out of the shallows after the break and stayed: pale, hunched walkers that range open country in pairs, a danger to anyone who leaves the roads. Rimefang packs and frost stalkers show differing adaptations to an expanding freeze. Cinder imps and lava-veined crabs live where human maintenance has become intermittent.

The Bog Mother is a successful animal in a damaged environment, not the supernatural mother of every lurker. Ash golems share the material history of the furnace works without every golem being a person. Grove apes, jade serpents and Bonegnaw make the jungle hazardous independent of its political disputes.

Hollows retain less coherent identity than returned tide-born. Drowned knights preserve an obligation with terrible fidelity. Watchers and riftlings are associated with the exposed break; their ultimate origin remains uncertain. Void hounds police the Lanternless's hoard, while abyss knights carry an older service beyond its intelligible end.

Bestiary combat hints remain specific and useful. If later lore changes an enemy's explanation, it must not contradict its actual block, parry, damage or movement behavior.

## 14. The investigation campaign

The archive is a parallel exploration progression, not a mandatory exposition gate for combat. Reading grants the existing one-time source XP. Completing an evidence set makes an account available to record in Journal → Investigations, granting a one-time reward. Reading a source again does not grant source XP again. Claimed accounts survive save/load for the active character.

| Investigation | Evidence path | Dramatic result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A shore without names | Wash wreck → Wren → founding stone | The refuge is made by returned people, not outsiders studying them |
| The price of a red bandana | Charter → brigand orders → unpaid ledger | An origin grievance and a current crime coexist |

Evidence may be read in any order. Existing characters receive credit for already-read source IDs. The final investigation requires the previous accounts to be recorded, not just their evidence found. The archive discloses locations for missing records while withholding their text and conclusions. Search must not reveal secret prose through matching.

The main quest separately requires the recorded defeat of Kaelen, the Warden and the Colossus plus three Fracture Cores. Completed old quests remain complete. Unfinished or stale ready states are rechecked against actual inventory and boss history.

## 15. Mysteries to preserve

**Established:** the release was withheld; the Duke was the last Anchor; the guardians were regulators; the ferryman collects for unfinished crossings.

**Supported interpretation:** the Hollow Man may have a missing entry in the manifest; rootwork pulses contribute to the Old King's fury; the passage's disruption produces tide-born memory loss.

**Open:** whose name was erased; whether the ferry ever had a wooden hull; the ultimate origin of the passage; where the Starborn fell from; the fate of the sky-fauna remembered by Drakelings; whether the original coast can ever be restored without sacrificing its current inhabitants.

Do not make every open question a secret boss or bloodline revelation. Some should become a better question; some should remain an absence people learn to live beside.

## 16. Future arcs — proposals, not implemented features

### The Relief Crew

After the Duke, Ignis and Brynja attempt a joint maintenance expedition. The player must secure tools, wages and a safe route, then choose which station receives the first complete crew. Difficulty comes from competing urgent needs and threats along the route. No option secretly condemns an unseen population without warning. The visual payoff would be workers occupying the previously empty tool hooks.

### The Returned Mask

Ama asks for a mediator after learning what her bounty stole. A Mossbound household will hear a restitution offer but will not accept silver as a substitute for a named mask. The player follows an item's actual provenance rather than collecting generic replacement masks. The endpoint is an acknowledged obligation, not instantaneous faction friendship.

### The Unmoored Port

A proposed offshore settlement made of repaired hulls rotates responsibility for its mooring. It offers a society organized around transferable work rather than inherited command. Its problem is participation: what happens when enough households refuse their turn? It should challenge the setting's values, not serve as a flawless answer to Saltreach.

### The Bell Under Ice

A proposed buried service station contains an earlier attempt to distribute command safely. Its records show a failure with no villain: a correct message arrived in a format the next station could not receive. This broadens the theme beyond finding a guilty ruler and introduces a dungeon built around routes, signals and reliable cooperation.

### Names Released

A proposed post-Lanternless story follows three incompatible reports of a returning light. None gives the player ownership of another person's identity. Resolve one local reunion; leave the cosmological destination uncertain. Keep the account focused on the people within the story.

## 17. Continuity checklist

Before adding content, ask:

1. Who wrote, made, maintained or paid for this thing?
2. What does the speaker know, and how did they learn it?
3. Is this a documented mechanic, a belief, or a future proposal?
4. Does a new claim preserve the chronology and existing character ages?
5. Does the encounter express a place's history without requiring a paragraph during combat?
6. Can the player inspect the consequence before an irreversible choice?
7. Does the reward support play without turning the archive into compulsory stat farming?
8. Have all source IDs, item rewards, NPC references and island placements been validated?
9. Does an old save still work without resetting its character?
10. What ordinary person benefits if the player succeeds?

The intended final image is not a hero standing above the world. It is a lamp kept lit by someone who now knows where the oil came from, who paid for it, and who was missing from the account.
